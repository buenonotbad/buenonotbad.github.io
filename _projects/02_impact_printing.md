Impact Printing is an additive manufacturing method that aggregates malleable soft particles through a robotic shooting process
([Ming et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1089/3dp.2021.0068)). Upon impact, the kinetic energy of each particle turns into plastic deformation, so the
particles bond through the material's own cohesion and self-interlocking, without any additional binder. Building on this method, our team designed and
fabricated a full-scale earthen wall with openings, using a custom shooting apparatus mounted on the lab's gantry robot, as part of the
[Impact Printed Structures](https://gramaziokohler.arch.ethz.ch/web/e/forschung/451.html) research at Gramazio Kohler Research.

The custom shooting apparatus is mounted on a gantry robot and digitally controlled. The mixed earthen material is loaded into a hopper and fed down to a
sensor-controlled wire cutter, which cuts it into soft particles. Two belts then accelerate each particle and shoot it onto the wall.

### Cracking

Shrinkage during drying makes cracks hard to avoid in earthen material. The V-shaped window frame, however, guides them into a single line below the frame,
instead of a distributed fracture pattern that could weaken the wall.

### Press

The Impact Printing research was later featured in architecture and additive-manufacturing media and won the 3D Pioneers Challenge 2023 (Architecture).
The process is now being brought to market by [Impact Build](https://www.impactbuild.ch/), a start-up that grew out of the research at ETH Zürich.
